Can Germany’s recovery outrun the AfD?

Issue: Sep 12, 2026 · 25–35 min
Europe · Economy & politics

Germany’s tentative recovery is colliding with industrial weakness and political anger.

Learning edition based on a topic selected from the Sep 12 Economist issue in the GitHub library. It preserves the argument and useful language without republishing the full copyrighted article.

Germany has spent years waiting for its economy to turn a corner. Growth is beginning to return, but the recovery remains too faint to transform the country’s political mood. Many voters do not experience an improving set of statistics as an improvement in daily life.

The problem is most visible in industry. Energy remains expensive, Chinese competition has intensified and carmakers are under pressure to cut costs. Volkswagen has argued that aligning its workforce with “economic reality is essential”. For workers in manufacturing towns, such language sounds less like a plan for renewal than a warning about jobs.

That gap between headline recovery and lived experience gives the Alternative for Germany room to grow. The party has prospered by presenting economic disruption, migration and distrust of established institutions as parts of the same story. A modest rise in output may therefore do little to reverse its momentum.

The central question is not simply whether Germany can grow again. It is whether growth can arrive quickly enough—and be felt widely enough—to restore confidence before political frustration hardens into lasting realignment.

Reading check

A strong answer

“Germany may be recovering on paper, but unless ordinary voters feel the benefits soon, economic growth may fail to halt the AfD’s political momentum.”

注意它没有复述所有细节，而是抓住 on paper vs lived experience 的冲突。

01

turn a corner

开始走出困境，出现转机。 它来自道路转弯的空间隐喻：过了弯，前方局面改变。新闻中常用于 economy, health, crisis。

02

lived experience

亲身经历、切身感受。 与抽象数据、政策叙事相对。它在社会科学和公共讨论中很常见，但使用过度时也可能显得空泛。

03

harden into

逐渐固化为。 harden 原本指物质变硬；用于态度、分歧和政治阵营时，暗示原本可改变的状态正在失去弹性。
